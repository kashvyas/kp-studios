Career case studyAmazon Web Services

Translating enterprise needs into cloud product adoption and ecosystem growth.

At AWS, Kash worked at the intersection of customers, partners, product and engineering—turning production requirements into roadmap input, launch priorities and adoption guidance.

RoleBusiness Development Manager
Period2011–2014
ServicesCloudFront · Route 53 · Media delivery
FocusCustomers · Partners · Product · Adoption

The operating challenge

Enterprise cloud adoption requires more than technical capability. Product teams need clear signals from real production users, while customers and partners need confidence in scalability, security, cost and operational fit.

What Kash worked on

  • Worked with AWS product and engineering teams across services including CloudFront, Route 53 and media-delivery solutions.
  • Translated enterprise customer requirements into roadmap input, launch priorities and adoption guidance.
  • Built customer and partner relationships around scalability, security and cost optimization for production workloads.
  • Connected market feedback with product positioning and ecosystem development.
  • Supported launches and adoption activity across a period of continued cloud-service growth.

Reported outcome from the role

Double-digitannual revenue growth reported during the period, with Kash supporting launches and ecosystem growth

What this case demonstrates

Strong product decisions often come from the boundary between the market and engineering. Customer needs have to be translated into specific product signals, while adoption requires credible guidance about how the technology behaves in real operating environments.

That experience informs VyasWorks work where a new technology, platform or market entry needs both product judgment and a practical adoption path.

Disclosure. This is a career case study describing work performed at Amazon Web Services, not a VyasWorks client engagement. Responsibilities and outcomes are drawn from Kash Vyas's supplied career materials.
